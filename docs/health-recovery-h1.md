# Persistent health recovery H1

H1 pauses routine exploration below an observed health threshold and preserves that pause across quiet turns. A recovering warrior no longer resumes its scouting role merely because the next turn sees it fortified. Explicit same-turn tactical orders may interrupt recovery for retreat or combat; they do not cancel the recovery state or replace the underlying mission. No settlement destination or production target is changed by this work.

## Policy and authority

`RecoveryPolicy` defaults to entering below 70% of observed maximum HP, resuming at 90%, and reconsidering after three completed own-turn intervals without observed net HP gain. These are adjustable heuristics: the 70% threshold catches the retained 35–50 HP exploration behavior while permitting minor damage; the 90% release threshold prevents repeated stop/resume near entry. No optimality or calibrated outcome probability is claimed. Configure through `StrategicController(recovery_policy=RecoveryPolicy(enter_below_percent=70, resume_at_percent=90, no_gain_turns=3))`; this H1 does not add YAML or model-selected policy parameters.

The overlay reads explicit `hp`, `max_hp` and `health_valid=true`. Unknown, invalid or missing health holds routine movement and cannot complete recovery. Native rows now derive HP and maximum from `GetDamage`/`GetMaxDamage`, validate integer bounds and carry an explicit invalid outcome instead of defaulting to 100 HP. Old eleven/twelve-field unit rows remain parseable without inventing a maximum or validity. New fourteen-field rows require either consistent valid numeric health or exact unknown tokens plus false validity. Exact health metadata remains owned-only; foreign units retain the existing HP bucket visibility rule and unknown buckets remain null.

Simulator unit observations annotate its source-defined 100 HP ceiling without mutating simulator state or its digest. Planner intake permits the strict optional valid-health extension, preserves the historical projection shape, and refuses malformed/unknown extended health instead of inventing a simulated healthy unit.

## Execution and persistence

`RecoveryTracker.begin_turn` proposes state from fresh projected observations. The controller commits only after successful turn closure; failed closure does not advance recovery history. A new loss or capture removes the unit from the owned recovery set. Healthy observations release a recovery state at the configured threshold; accepted orders alone never do.

Already fortified recovering units retain their standing order without another scouting action. Otherwise the controller sends at most one ordinary facade fortify request. The 16-unit execution limit, at-most-two scouting attempts, frozen-movement authority, ownership/visible-target guards, provider limits, watchdog and closure checks remain in force. Recovery needing an order has priority behind explicit tactical tasks, and graph audits retain deferred-unit counts. If a later refreshed read first reveals unknown/damaged health, the next routine decision holds that unit and retains its newly discovered recovery state after closure.

The recovery overlay never replaces the directive's scouting roles or future settlement mission identity. Explicit move/attack/hold/found overrides retain their existing single-turn semantics. A reviewed tactical retreat can therefore happen while recovery remains pending on the following turn. Rejection and unavailable targets do not relax existing engine/facade authority.

New damage during recovery joins the existing strategy-request reasons. Three intervals without observed gain add one reconsideration for that no-gain streak; positive observed gain starts a new streak. All reasons share the existing one strategic decision opportunity and its format-repair/request caps. There is no per-unit provider loop. Quiet recovery turns do not request the model merely because HP remains below threshold; normal configured strategic cadence still applies.

Existing strategy execution/graph events carry the policy, health, recovery transitions and action observations. Model metadata includes compact recovery rows; curated own-unit context and change packets carry health validity and maximum. The controller remains fresh-only: these audit records are not resume checkpoints.
